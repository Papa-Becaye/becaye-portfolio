## Start with the task, not the model

The useful question is not "which model should we use?" but "which decision needs assistance?". In a product, AI does not replace a feature: it speeds up a task the user already recognises.

## Three concrete constraints

- **Scope**: an agent should have few tools, clearly described.
- **Data**: without reliable context, even the smoothest answer stays fragile.
- **Verification**: any critical output must be confirmable by a person.

> An agent's autonomy is often less valuable than its ability to ask for confirmation.

## What I am exploring

I am testing these principles in business contexts: summarising site documents, extracting structured information and suggesting progress states. The goal is not to add AI everywhere, but to keep a predictable interface where users understand what happened and why.

For a Full Stack developer, AI becomes an architectural component like any other: it has inputs, outputs, costs and limits. Treating it with the same rigour as the rest of the system is the best way to get something durable from it.
