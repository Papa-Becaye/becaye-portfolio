## The trap of the brilliant solution

I am a developer: my reflex is to build. But an elegant architecture does not guarantee a useful product. The real work starts when you can explain the problem in one sentence and identify who is suffering from it.

## What I changed in my method

1. Listen before drawing screens.
2. Describe a user's full journey, not a list of features.
3. Measure real usage rather than the amount of code produced.

> A good product simplifies a working day. A great product becomes hard to replace.

## The developer-founder role

Being both technical and entrepreneurial is an advantage: it shortens the gap between an idea and a testable version. It is also a responsibility: you must be able to say no to a tempting feature that is off-topic.

So I keep one simple rule: every week, something must reach a user's hands. Not an internal demo, not documentation — a real piece of product. That constraint is what turns an idea into a company.
